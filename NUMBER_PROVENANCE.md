# Number provenance

Every number quoted in `docs/PLAN_ASSESSMENT.md` traces to a CSV generated by a script in
this repo, and `verify_paper_numbers.py` re-derives all of them on every `bash plotter.sh`.
Adding a number to a document means adding it to that checker's `DERIVED` list.

Nothing here is hand-computed. Statistics are stored at 6 dp so that a printed value is
rounded exactly once: 2 dp storage turned a true 68.2479 into 68.25 into a wrong 68.3 in a
sibling repo. Printing uses round-half-**up**, not Python's banker's default.

## Definitions that a reader could otherwise get wrong

- **`best_cer`** — the minimum CER over a run's 101 evaluations. This is the **primary**
  metric. `final_cer` is the last evaluation and is secondary: mean `final_minus_best` is
  3.47, so the last checkpoint is frequently not the best one.
- **Aggregation population.** Every aggregate in `PLAN_ASSESSMENT.md` is computed over
  **finished runs of the four grid-wide variants** (`earth, fire, global, water`), excluding
  the runs listed in `EXCLUDED_MODELS_AGGREGATE`. `base` and `Qwen3-4B` exist only for
  `crs_sc`, so including them would compare a 10-language mean against a 1-language mean.
  (An earlier draft of the assessment mixed the two populations and quoted 0.96 where the
  core-variant median is 1.03; the checker caught it.)
- **`audio_hours`** — cumulative audio **processed**, counting repeats, derived from
  `train/train_audio_seconds`, which sums decoded array length over sampling rate per batch.
  It is **not** unique corpus hours. Where unique hours are needed they are reported as
  `implied_stream_hours` in `t4`, always with an `estimate_kind` of `estimate` or
  `lower_bound`: under streaming the epoch counter records how many times the stream was
  consumed, so the division is only meaningful once the stream has wrapped at least once
  (`en_us` and `hi_in` sit at exactly 1.000 and are lower bounds only).
- **`expected_stream_examples`** — the sum of a language's training configs' `num_examples`,
  from the HuggingFace dataset builder metadata (frozen in
  `utils.WORLDSPEECH_TRAIN_EXAMPLES`). Summing is valid because interleaving is lossless.
  This **replaced** an hours snapshot taken from a summarised reading of the WorldSpeech
  paper. That snapshot was the weakest input in the analysis and it produced a false
  data-integrity finding for Tamil; numbers recovered from prose should not gate a flag.
- **`t4` is not a data-integrity check.** A ratio away from 1.0 is a bookkeeping observation
  about the run, not evidence of corrupt or missing data. Integrity is checked only by
  `verify_dataset_durations.py`, against the real datasets.

- **`stream_post_filter`** — the volume axis. Pre-filter example counts minus each config's
  at-cap fraction (`utils.CONFIG_DURATION_AT_CAP`), because the strict `< 30 s` cap removes
  clips before the model sees them. For `ta_in` that is 32,107 → 8,846. Comparing against the
  *pre*-filter count is what made Tamil look like a data-integrity problem.
- **Why `ta_in` is not excluded.** The region-match contrast is within-language: every variant
  saw the identical stream, so the loss cannot bias it. Excluding the language would remove the
  only low-resource cell, which is the point of the headline analysis.
- **Absolute vs relative effect.** Both are reported. The absolute Δ correlates with volume at
  rho = 0.964; the relative Δ (as % of baseline CER) only at rho = 0.714, p = 0.071. Volume and
  baseline CER are collinear (rho = -0.679), so the two candidate explanations are entangled and
  the partial correlation is reported for both the full set (inconclusive) and the six
  non-extreme languages (significant).

## Known-unverified inputs

- **Whether the volume decay is driven by data volume or by task difficulty.** They are
  collinear across these languages and the partial correlation is inconclusive over all seven.
  Only a within-language volume manipulation can separate them — re-running Tamil at full volume
  while keeping the low-volume runs.
- The expected stream size for `crs_sc`, which trains on the `ERISLab/WorldSpeech` mirror
  (`train_val_exc_clean`); that split is not in the example-count snapshot.
- The matched-variant premise (identical base/tokenizer/parameter count across the four
  regional variants), still unconfirmed against the Tiny Aya report.

## Settled, do not re-report

- **Interleaving does not oversample the smaller config.** The loader uses
  `all_exhausted_without_replacement`, so a combined stream is exactly the sum of its parts.
  Proved offline in `verify_interleave_semantics.py` (runs in `plotter.sh`) and confirmed on
  the real `ta_in` + `ta_lk` splits with `verify_dataset_durations.py --load`.
- **The Tamil configs contain no corrupt or mislabelled clips.** The duration-consistency
  filter removes **zero** samples from `ta_in` + `ta_lk`, so the corpus `duration` column
  agrees with the decoded audio. The Tamil data loss was caused by the *duration cap*, not by
  data quality.
- **`ta_in`'s reconstructed stream matches one config because the cap removed the other.**
  Resolved: 100 % of `ta_lk` sits at exactly 30.00 s and the cap is strict. The epoch-counter
  reconstruction was accurate throughout (8,825 inferred vs 8,846 true).
- **The `crs_sc` `_clean` splits are all genuinely cleaned** — train, val and test carry the
  same duration-consistency filter. The committed upstream example only demonstrates it on
  test, which is why it can look otherwise.
